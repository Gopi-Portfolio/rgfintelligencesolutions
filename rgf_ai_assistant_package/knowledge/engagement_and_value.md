# Engagement and Business Value

Status: Draft for RGF approval before production ingestion.

## How an engagement can begin

RGF starts with the business problem rather than a predetermined technology. An initial conversation can cover the current process, pain points, users, existing systems, available data, constraints, risks, and the result the organization wants to achieve.

## Typical engagement logic

1. Clarify the problem and desired outcome.
2. Understand the current workflow, systems, data, users, and constraints.
3. Identify and prioritize feasible opportunities.
4. Agree on scope, success measures, risks, and responsibilities.
5. Validate the solution through an appropriately bounded proof, pilot, or implementation plan.
6. Measure results, collect feedback, and decide whether to adjust, stop, or scale.

This is a description of how RGF may structure work; it is not a fixed commercial promise or mandatory sequence for every customer.

## How value is addressed

Before scaling, RGF can work with the customer to define measures such as time saved, reduced rework, response speed, completion rate, data quality, adoption, customer experience, revenue opportunity, or risk reduction. The relevant measures depend on the business problem.

## If the solution does not create value

RGF does not promise that every idea will produce value. The purpose of discovery, measurable success criteria, bounded validation, and review checkpoints is to test assumptions early. If evidence does not support the expected benefit, the parties can adjust the approach, narrow or change the use case, or avoid scaling an unsuitable solution.

## How to engage

Invite the visitor to use the website’s **Tell us your business problem** page. Do not invent pricing, delivery dates, contract terms, or availability.
